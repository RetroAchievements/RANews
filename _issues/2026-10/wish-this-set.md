* Write-up by: {% rauserpic speedruntrainer %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">

Wishing this set as this is the most fun vanilla experience there is. At the start of the game you choose the difficulty. The difficulty determines how likely it is to get negative or positive cards. When you get a star in a level, you can choose a card. Red is a negative effect, and green is a positive effect. For example, negative effects can be Mario getting killed in 1 hit, Sonic mode, 20 FPS, some weird camera effects, and many more. But you can get positive cards also, such as fewer coins required to get the 100 coin star, get a free random star, etc. There are so many cards in the game. The expiration of a card means how many stars you get until an effect gets deactivated. So this means many effects will add over time! Many achievements could be created, like getting X amount of stars with X negative or even positive effects, playing on different difficulties, and much more!

## Attack of the Movies 3-D (Wii)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Console | Genre                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/46641" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/168381.png" alt="Attack of the Movies 3-D"> <span>Attack of the Movies 3-D</span></a> | Wii     | [Rail Shooter](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5374) |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Whynot15 %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://images.launchbox-app.com//92529d95-3c3b-45ef-948a-b0d77e2a5516.png">

Attack of the Movies 3-D is a game some people might deride as "shovelware". I won't say that's completely wrong, but this game has a weird place in my heart and really exemplifies the coziness of the Wii library.

Attack of the Movies 3-D is a lightgun shooter with a B-movie theme. You take on 6 stages - a mutant insect invasion, a space battle, an ancient tomb, an underwater sea lab, a post-robot apocalypse city, and finally a haunted graveyard - and blast away at whatever the enemies de jure are. And...that's really it. The game isn't particularly difficult, each stage takes about 5-6 minutes to play, and the graphics are decent but nothing mindblowing. But...hear me out here...it's just fun. Whether you're taking out space ships or zombies or mutant ants, it's a blast running through the levels in all their B-movie cheesiness. They even have the titular 3D feature, with the help of those cheap blue and red glasses anyone who grew up in the 90s knows too well. And one of the coolest things - it supports up to 4 players! A rarity when it comes to console light gun shooters.

This game won't change your life or your entire outlook on gaming, but it's an enjoyable, albeit short, romp, almost like a version of a Universal Studio theme park ride, that deserves a fun little set on here - maybe even with multiplayer?

## Jazz Jackrabbit (Game Boy Advance)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Console          | Genre                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/10364" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/075059.png" alt="Jazz Jackrabbit"> <span>Jazz Jackrabbit</span></a> | Game Boy Advance | [2D Platforming](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8837), [Run & Gun](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7669) |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpicalt JenniferAmber, justyellow %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/368/368929486/e21fd19391ac638897cd34ca209b1413fef0a22e/backfill-7kmke7coqe6u3YfjTIL.png">
<figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

Developed by [Game Titan](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7542) and published by [Jaleco](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5147) in 2002, Jazz Jackrabbit for the GBA brings Epic Games’ PC mascot back after 4 years. Rather than porting any of the classic titles, though, this is an odd reboot of sorts. It gives Jazz a sci-fi spin against a new nemesis, Dark Shell, complete with RPG-style intermissions, planetary travel, and a whole shop system where you buy extra lives and ammunition using money collected in stages.

The gameplay adds a few neat features to the platformer run-and-gun formula, including directional aiming and Carrot Grenades reminiscent of something like [Metal Slug](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5798). However, it definitely comes with its fair share of flaws. The weapons skew more towards realistic guns (like replacing his iconic default gun with a basic Uzi-looking gun), the stages can get a bit monotonous, especially because the soundtrack is nothing like the iconic PC bangers, it sounds like generic handheld songs that always loop a bit too early. The camera is zoomed in close enough to make fast platforming a bit hazardous, but it's not a game that can be played as fast as Jazz 2 can, even if it was more zoomed-out.

It's not a GREAT game, but it remains a fascinating piece of handheld history. It even had a surprisingly robust 4-player Link Cable multiplayer mode! A set would be a great way to give this kinda unique reboot the attention it missed out on.

## <span class="tag"><span>Prototype</span></span> Space Fantasy Zone (PC Engine CD/TurboGrafx-CD)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Console                    | Genre                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/13245" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/000001.png" > <span><span class="tag"><span>Prototype</span></span> Space Fantasy Zone</span></a> | PC Engine CD/TurboGrafx-CD | [Action](https://retroachievements.org/hub/836), [Rail Shooter](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5374), [Shoot ‘em Up](https://retroachievements.org/hub/952) |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Frenchy70 %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/159/1599040206/e1b856849c2ec69e3b66beadd83b0d2af770fdbb/e1b856849c2ec69e3b66beadd83b0d2af770fdbb.png">
<figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

Ever wish that [Space Harrier](https://retroachievements.org/game/2327) had a good soundtrack and had a crossover spin off with [Fantasy Zone](https://retroachievements.org/hub/6558)? That was almost the case for the PCCD in 1995, but was unfathomably cancelled at the last moment. The game was considered a lost oddity for years until a 95% complete build was leaked online in 2008.

You play as the space ship Opa-Opa, and shoot your way through a mix of Space Harrier and Fantasy Zone enemies in stages from the latter. The music consists of remixes of Fantasy Zone and Space Harrier songs in sweet, sweet CD quality audio.

There are 8 levels, with lives being replaced with “Shields”. You earn money by shooting down enemies, with a bonus if you shoot down an entire formation. The terrain is no longer destructible, so you need to dodge obstacles more so than in Space Harrier, and Opa-Opa is on the faster side with its movement.

The bosses are the same ones from Fantasy Zone, but have their patterns translated to the Space Harrier format in some creative ways, along with getting a facelift with their sprites. They also now have a timer, counting down until they’ll run away.

After each stage, Opa-Opa goes to Weponald’s to buy more shields and single use super attacks. There’s even an ending if you make it through all 8 stages. This is a real hidden gem that NEC produced, and a real shame it never released. Maybe Sega cancelled the project to not have it upstage their Sega CD games. In either case, this would make a fun set and is almost entirely in English, so it’s easy to pick up and play.

## Phantasy Star Online Episode III: C.A.R.D. Revolution (GameCube)

| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic mimicmatter %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/165/1650420430/3df99f0a9b6921d1a1e988f434b37b57a5c35115/3df99f0a9b6921d1a1e988f434b37b57a5c35115-lg-webp.webp">

Who has played [Phantasy Star Online](https://retroachievements.org/game/10828) on the Dreamcast? Who has played the updated version [Phantasy Star Online Episode 1 & 2](https://retroachievements.org/game/5404) on the Nintendo Gamecube?

[Phantasy Star](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8541) is SEGA's attempt at making a popular RPG game that would rival [Final Fantasy](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7221). Setting up a hi-tech futuristic theme, the series would initially spawn 4 turn-based RPG games.

Phantasy Star Online was built more as an Action RPG, and as the name suggests, heavily enforced the online play. However, it was totally possible to play locally with up to 4 players at a time. In any case, it was a multiplayer experience.

And then Episode 3 came out, and it’s a CARD GAME!

Yeah, not sure why [Sonic Team](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8080) decided to turn the franchise into a card game, but they sure did, and most people who loved the franchise, myself included, were a bit disappointed in this game. Years later I have to say, while it is not what makes Phantasy Star an interesting franchise, as a card game it’s really interesting.

You play as a Commander, leading the characters into a fight with members of a rival organization. You can play as Hunters, working with the government of Pioneer 2 that uses weapons and armor, or as Arkz, working against the current government leadership that summons creatures found on the Planet Ragol to fight by their side. You roll dice, set up your cards, and move your character and creatures on a grid map to attack your opponent and eliminate them before they eliminate you. While you can play online or offline with friends, the game also offers an offline story mode, setting up the story of the game with quests of various difficulty.

Developers out there that really are into card games or Phantasy Star enjoyers, I ask you to look into this game, still without a set, hoping it sparks the interest of someone.

## NBA Jam (Wii)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Console | Genre                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | --------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/35827" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/000001.png" alt="NBA Jam"> <span>NBA Jam</span></a> | Wii     | [Sports - Basketball] |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Kingsombra %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://images.launchbox-app.com//2bee167c-5278-4355-87f8-5a3cb03503de.jpg">
<figcaption></figcaption>

When this game was first announced, I was very worried that [EA](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7121) would just use it as a nostalgia cashgrab and make a very mediocre product. Boy, was I wrong. EA built a full retail game around the Midway formula - 2-on-2, no fouls, turbo, alley-oops, and Tim Kitzrow yelling “BOOMSHAKALAKA!” - then split it in half and gave us 2 different campaigns.

Classic Campaign is what you expect from the [original game](https://retroachievements.org/game/593): pick a team from the 2010-11 NBA, beat the rest of the league, and finish against Larry Bird and Magic Johnson. But the Remix Tour is where the real magic happens. Smash turns the backboards into health bars. Elimination kicks the lowest scorer after every round. Domination plays hot-spot streetball. 21 and Remix 2v2 dump power-ups on the floor. Boss battles twist those rules around legends.

The game has its own achievements list called "Jam Challenges", so any developer working on it would already have a good idea on what to put into a set. And with these challenges, you get unlocks, cheats, bosses, and more. You can play as mascots, the Beastie Boys, and even Democrats or Republicans.

The NBA Jam remake is like the same old (but great) joke, but with a bigger and better punchline. Everything that you loved about the 90s original is here, but it's all bigger now. So come on and slam, and welcome to the Jam.

Alter Echo is a weird, quirky game that seems like a bit of a trip when you play it, but there's truly nothing else like it that I can find in this era of gaming. It's a platformer beat-em-up where your character can take 3 different forms: a fast, agile melee attacker, a heavy, slow moving ranged attacker, and a frail, slippery stealth mover. You can swap between these forms at will, making the most of each ability as you traverse levels that look like they were pulled out of a tie-dye factory mishap while a pile of sentient goo with daddy issues asks you to help stop him before he takes over the galaxy. I never beat it as a kid, but I remember liking it a lot, so imagine my surprise when the game was in RA's database, but my disappointment when there were no achievements for it.

With the ability to swap between forms at will, there's plenty of room for challenges. Getting entire rooms done with the stealth suit, doing stealth as the big guy, getting through a combat melee only without taking damage... I haven't played the game in quite a while, but I'm sure the possibilities are very numerous. It is, at the time of writing, sitting at a pretty 40 requests (one of which is my own), so I know there's interest in it. I'm sure whoever takes it will have a good time figuring out how best to give us a challenge as we try to help the Echoplast.

## Icebreaker (3DO Interactive Multiplayer)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Console                     | Genre  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/7116" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/037153.png" alt="Icebreaker"> <span>Icebreaker</span></a> | 3DO Interactive Multiplayer | Action |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic gamerforlifeforever %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/108/1084713678/0a9236ae7c7814da38e808678e5b4c10577b4999/backfill-cbnme83t66t75jJJThC.png">
<figcaption></figcaption>

The optometrist office I went to as a kid had a Panasonic 3DO with a game which had a bunch of triangles in it, but I couldn’t remember the name of it until I did some searching. I eventually remembered that game’s name is Icebreaker. You would think with a name like Icebreaker you’d have to break ice, but nope, it’s just triangles.

You play as a white triangle, and you have to clear out all the other triangles to move onto the next level. Out of the stationary triangles, you have blue ones which you can just crash through, red ones you have to shoot, and green triangles which only your enemies can break. From what I recall in later levels, there’s also purple triangles which turn into a giant hole when shot, and rainbow triangles which have the effect of one of the other triangles depending on when you shoot them. Yellow triangles are your enemies. They can move, hide behind other triangles, and you have to avoid bumping into them.

Overall, I remember having fun with this for the limited time that I could play it, and would love to see it get a set so that I could get the motivation to play it again.

## Dodgeball (PlayStation 2)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Console       | Genre              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/27510" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/000001.png" alt="Dodgeball"> <span>Dodgeball</span></a> | PlayStation 2 | Sports - Dodgeball |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Carca %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/515/515861198/d4276d586a0efb615c7e9d8255c68ef296354c80/d4276d586a0efb615c7e9d8255c68ef296354c80-lg-webp.webp">

German speakers call it the People's Ball. French speakers call it the Prisoner's Ball. Swedish call it Ghost Ball. Japanese call it Sniper. Latin America call it Burned. Portuguese calls it KILL!

English speakers call it Dodgeball. Something was lost in the translation.

But most countries have the same common ground: the game our PE tutors would make us play when feeling particularly sadistic on that particular day. The goal, in case your teenage trauma has suppressed this memory, is to eliminate the adversary's team by hitting them with a ball. Hard. Muscles got bruised, the occasional nose got bloodied, one or two teeth got chipped. It was war. Sure, there were winners, but at what cost?

Developed by [Access](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8202) and published by [D3](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5266), Dodgeball is a surprisingly fun PS2 game. In this outing, you enter a World Championship and make your way into becoming the Ultimate Cross-Continental Cross-Eras Dodgeball champion, forever! The graphics are cute and well elaborated, the specials are very cool to pull off, and you can feel the devastating blow of a well aimed attack. Every team is comprised of historical figures, which adds a comedic touch to the experience. Ever wondered if 18th century AD's composer Beethoven could beat 5th century BC's philosopher Socrates in a dodgeball match? Wonder no more, get your hands on this game and find out yourself!
