## Linkle Liver Story (Saturn)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Console | Genre                                                |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/14643" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/027441.png" alt="Linkle Liver Story"> <span>Linkle Liver Story</span></a> | Saturn  | [Action RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8625) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic MGNS8M %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic PepeJohn %}

I'd like to recommend Linkle Liver Story for all RA players, especially those who like to dig through lesser-known games. Exploring the Saturn library is always fun to do, and this game is a pretty good one to showcase the console's style. The presentation is definitely the highlight here, with very charming sprites, cool environments, animations and, well, just overall vibes. Apparently it was initially conceived as a sequel to [Crusader of Centy](https://retroachievements.org/game/1438), which is a better game in my opinion, but those who played it know just what to expect from this one.

The combat is serviceable and the dungeons feel varied enough. The game being on the shorter side also helps it to never become repetitive.
One of the weirder aspects is how you get new weapons. Throughout the game, you'll find elemental seeds to plant that'll allow you to raise different weapon types as well as make them stronger. Different weapons, of course, have different reach, speed, and attack power, enabling you to tailor the gameplay to an extent.

The set, made by {% rauserpic MGNS8M %}, is pretty neat, and going for the mastery made the experience even more fun. Since Linkle Liver Story is a really easy game, having boss challenges actually demanding effort made the victories all the more satisfying. It's just a simple, well-made set that has just the right degree of difficulty, never feeling unfair.

## Poinie's Poin (PlayStation 2)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Console       | Genre                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/22870" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/069795.png" alt="Poinie's Poin"> <span>Poinie's Poin</span></a> | PlayStation 2 | [3D Platforming](https://retroachievements.org/hub/2952) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Layton %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic CheeseKnight %}

I'm not sure Poinie's Poin can be accurately described within the constraints of the English language, but I'll give it my best shot.

Poinie's Poin is a Japan-exclusive PS2 adventure platformer released in 2002, an era where game developers could try out some wild ideas in their games. Developer Alvion leaned into the absurd to create a fever dream of a game that is as entertaining as it is nonsensical. The characters are weird, the dialogue is weird, the plot is weird, everything about this game is a trip in the best way.

You are Poinie, a young child who has lost their mother in Jellytown. As you explore the city and surrounding areas trying to find her, you encounter a larger nefarious plot and must save the day. The main mechanic of the game is poins, round bubbles that come in three colors and affect the user's emotions. Red poins make things angry, blue poins make things sad, and yellow poins create happiness. You use the poins to manipulate the environment around you and defeat enemies. This creates unique boss battles where in place of traditional attacks dealing damage, you need to figure out which combination of emotions solve the puzzle to defeat your opponent.

Despite not seeing a Western release, the game has full English voice acting and dialogue, which really adds to the game's charm. The cast of characters you meet on your journey is whimsical and eccentric, from the Meanies, a schoolyard gang that's more bark than bite, to your sarcastic, sometimes profane sidekick (also named Poin). Even though it's a brightly colored game that seems aimed at a wide audience, there's a number of out of pocket lines of dialogue that will make you do a double take. It's a bizarre adventure that will leave you wondering what the heck you just played, but glad you went along for the ride.

## JoJo's Bizarre Adventure: Heritage for the Future (Arcade)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Console | Genre                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/11817" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/154359.png" alt="JoJo's Bizarre Adventure: Heritage for the Future"> <span>JoJo's Bizarre Adventure: Heritage for the Future</span></a> | Arcade  | [2D Fighting](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8260) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Hexadigital %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Prota %}

If you're in the mood for some anime fighting game goodness, I'd recommend giving JoJo's Bizarre Adventure: Heritage for the Future a shot. It's an updated version of [JoJo's Venture](https://retroachievements.org/game/15712), featuring a new mode, characters, moves, and more.

The game looks and plays great, with an awesome presentation as well, but the real highlight is the stand mechanic. Each character can summon a stand, which either serves as an active summonable creature or weapon that changes the properties of your moveset and, in the case of the creatures, can even be controlled independently. A unique aspect of the gameplay is developing a playstyle that incorporates taking advantage of the differences between keeping your stand active or not and knowing when you should weave between the states. The set is designed to have players try out the entire cast in each mode, with mastery leading to the completion of both modes on the hardest difficulty with everyone.

It's one of the more unique fighters out there, as is the anime it is based on. Trying out the game for [The Unwanted](https://retroachievements.org/forums/topic/14787) not only made me a fan of the game, but also dive into the anime, and I've fallen in love with that as well. Any JoJo fan should absolutely give it a try, and I'd say the same for 2D fighting game fans who haven't had the chance to check it out yet.

## SpongeBob SquarePants: Creature from the Krusty Krab (Game Boy Advance)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Console          | Genre                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/7698" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/113915.png" alt="SpongeBob SquarePants: Creature from the Krusty Krab"> <span>SpongeBob SquarePants: Creature from the Krusty Krab</span></a> | Game Boy Advance | [2D Platforming](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8837), [Action-Adventure](https://retroachievements.org/hub/2652) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic electricangel %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Sniwott %}

[SpongeBob](https://retroachievements.org/hub/9038) needs no introduction. It's a highly prominent TV series that has spun off into multiple spin-off shows, movies, and video games, quickly becoming a staple of many people's childhoods. One of the games created was none other than SpongeBob SquarePants: Creature from the Krusty Krab, which had a few different versions depending on what console you were on. If you were playing the [PS2](https://retroachievements.org/game/19543) or Wii versions, you'd get a 3D platformer. If you were playing the DS version, you'd get a 2D platformer with touch screen controls. If you were playing the GBA version, however, you'd get a highly varied 2D action game with multiple gameplay styles, making for a very fun game. Better yet, it has an amazing RA set to go with it!

CftKK’s RA set has a bunch of neat achievements, ranging from tests of skill and memory to mechanical challenges. For example, [I See You... Zap!](https://retroachievements.org/achievement/445572) requires keeping all of the enemies in mind, with precision blasting needed in order to defeat them before they can destroy any part of the bridge. This makes for a fun and chaotic achievement where you'll have to aim your reticle in advance to be able to defeat the enemies quickly enough, and while you're doing that, you also have to pay attention to the enemies in the background as well. Another good achievement is [The Sticky Goo of My Own Folley](https://retroachievements.org/achievement/445569), which asks you to complete a chase sequence without landing in any of the goo below you. This makes for a fun challenge where you need to hurry up to not get knocked into the goo by the Krabby Patty chasing you, all while jumping from rope to rope and avoiding condiment projectiles. Finally, [Hands At Ten and Two and Finally... Floor It!](https://retroachievements.org/achievement/445587) wants you to find the most optimal route for the final stage of the game. After a bit of trial and error along with some memorization and practice, it isn't too bad to pull off, and it ends up being a pretty satisfying experience once you find that perfect route.

Overall, the GBA version of CftKK is an underrated GBA gem, with great music, stylish graphics, and fun and varied gameplay styles. The achievement set that it currently has helps to enhance the experience, and by the time you're done with it, I'm certain that you'll come out loving the game just like I did!

## Eternal Ring (PlayStation 2)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Console       | Genre                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/2723" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/061402.png" alt="Eternal Ring"> <span>Eternal Ring</span></a> | PlayStation 2 | [Action RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8625) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic malasdair %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic ABCDEFGHI %}

This must be [FromSoftware](https://retroachievements.org/hub/3134)’s crew trying to test the limits of the newly released PS2 to see how much of the original [King's Field](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8277) series’ ideas could be brought up there. Maybe that's why this spiritual successor wasn't as popular as the original trilogy or why they avoided calling this a King’s Field game despite having all the same tropes, including the poisoned area.

Many reviews I’ve seen say it's too barebones and too linear. I welcome the change, because there were many mandatory mazelike structures in the original [KF1](https://retroachievements.org/game/14050) that leave you lost without a map. I think they were trying to avoid mazelike sections, as the only zones in the game like that (Cliff Forest and the Sealed Labyrinth) are optional.

Besides all of the critiques, it's safe to say that magic is OP here, so you don't have to use close attacks that much, which was 70% of your deaths in KF. Instead, use a Wind attack ring and a health ring, and you are set for pretty much the entire game. I feel it's overlooked how much they improved the experience over the original game, even in the minor things like actually going faster and having a dynamic sword attack. They even put low level crabs in the beginning beach area to easily increase your level, instead of a skeleton that 1 hit KOs you. The game doesn't overstay its welcome; it throws pretty much everything at you from the beginning, and if you already know what you have to do, you can clear it in [a few in-game days](https://retroachievements.org/achievement/253334).

The OST is okay for what it's worth. Some tracks feel generic, but the boss theme is the best theme. I don't know if it's the bass or what, but it feels so good. The voices are on par with [Doctor Wahwee](https://retroachievements.org/game/11365), but since I don't care for any story in these types of games, the "so bad it’s good" trope works in their favor. Like, how do you expect me to take Soulus seriously with that voice? I mean, I know it's a baby, but still, it's too funny.

The achievement set, not counting the speedrun or the [pseudo minimalist rings](https://retroachievements.org/achievement/253333) run, is designed to make you pretty much try everything this game has to offer. On one hand, it's a good design choice to make you try everything, from stealing the optional stiletto to using the attribution rings to not awaken the gargoyles, but completing the ring collection can become much of a slog due to the randomness of attribution ring creation. But even so, there is a guide by BitsAndGlory that shows how to create all rings (and all of the non-progression achievements if you don't know how to do them). So, there isn't much stopping from the mastery if you actually enjoy the gameplay.

## Parasite Eve (PlayStation)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Console     | Genre                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/11277" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/108237.png" alt="Parasite Eve"> <span>Parasite Eve</span></a> | PlayStation | [Action RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8625), [Survival Horror](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8328) |

* Set by: {% rauserpicalt tele, televandalist %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic kalonZombie %}

I had never played Parasite Eve before, so when looking for a game to play for Halloween time, I settled on it because I knew it was a horror-bent JRPG with an interesting battle mechanic. Combat takes place in real-time, with you able to avoid enemy attacks as you wait for your ATB bar to fill and do your own attacks. Your attack radius is shown as you make your moves, allowing you to know if you'll be hitting things or not, which makes positioning important as well. If an enemy is too far you'll simply miss, and if they move out of your radius when you begin attacking while they're in it you'll end up dealing damage at a greatly reduced rate. While you have to use ammo, it's plentiful enough that you'll never really have to worry about it. The ammo is more to make sure you have to reload your weapons every so often.

The set to go along with the game is very good, too. There are very few missables (which is a plus in my book when it comes to doing sets) outside some boss challenges. There are also a few optional bosses thrown in, a challenge here or there, but the bulk of the post-game set is in the Chrysler Building, a huge megadungeon that allows you to truly build busted weapon and armor combinations. Most importantly, though, the set respects your time. While you do have to grind for one of the superweapons, that's just it... it's only one of the superweapons, and it's dealer's choice. Otherwise, the roughest part of the set is the Chrysler Building, but that's less the set's fault and more of how much of a gauntlet meatgrinder the Chrysler Building is.

So yeah, game's good, set's good. If you're looking for something spooky-adjacent that's not actually too scary, this is a great one to give a whirl. I went from never touching the game to playing it twice in a row for the mastery. Have fun!

## Megabots (Apple II)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Console  | Genre                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/14024" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/059567.png" alt="Megabots"> <span>Megabots</span></a> | Apple II | [Roguelike](https://retroachievements.org/hub/3321) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic caricatur %}, {% rauserpic Etron %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Whynot15 %}

Megabots is an oddball game perfectly at home on the Apple II. It's a simple old school dungeon crawler mixed with a blind version of rock paper scissors and a hint of resource management and memorization. The premise is fairly simple - you pick 2 of 4 weapons before sending your robot down four dungeon-like levels. Your goal is to get to the exit on each of the first three floors, then locate a power core at the end of the fourth and final floor.

Each floor is filled to the brim with other droids. Some are friendly - if you talk to them, they can give you hints about the floor (which may or may not be accurate) and even give you a much-needed boost of energy! But others are itching for a fight, and only by using the right weapon (at least somewhat randomly determined each game) will you be able to survive and potentially take some energy for yourself. By only having two weapons, that means you'll often run into bots you can't defeat, forcing you to make a hasty retreat to survive and find a new route to the end of the floor. Don't blast too quickly though - take down a friendly and you'll be penalized with energy loss!

That's the game in a nutshell. Runs are fairly quick affairs where you need to hope you run into good events for more power/power credits and aren't forced into unwinnable fights that drain you too quickly. It shares some similarities with [Minesweeper](https://retroachievements.org/hub/21371), where you'll need to determine moves that give you the best odds of survival while ultimately leaving your fate entirely up to chance. It can be quite frustrating to roll runs where you get crushed from the onset, or worse, have a solid start completely dismantled due to a string of bad encounters. Just the same, it's quite a thrill when you manage to get the perfect series of encounters that set you up for a game-winning run.

It's not the most in-depth game you'll find, even on the Apple II, but give it a try and you'll find some good fun blasting droids and clearing out floors!

## <span class="tag"><span>Hack</span></span> Hatsune Miku in Sonic 1 (Genesis/Mega Drive)

This is an interesting [Sonic 1](https://retroachievements.org/game/1) hack which replaces the popular hedgehog with Hatsune Miku. While it looks like a simple sprite swap, as you play you will find Mikudayo dolls hidden in each level, sometimes with small changes like a fake wall or an extra platform that weren't there in the original.

However, the main attraction to play this set is to play as Mikudayo, an unlockable mode which replaces Miku with the aforementioned character. You could say this is akin to a hard mode, because Mikudayo moves a lot more slowly and does not spin. Depending on the route you take, you may get softlocked, since in a few levels spinning is necessary to progress.

The set is pretty fun, and while controlling Mikudayo may take some time to get used to, the challenges in the set do not feel frustrating at all, mostly requiring knowledge about each level layout in advance and careful planning, a perfect challenge for Sonic fans. And besides, it is Miku. What other reason do you need to try this hack?

I am honestly very impressed by all the effort this hacker/dev has put in. More people deserve to give these Donkey Kong hacks more of a spotlight.

## Victorious Boxers: Revolution (Wii)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Console | Genre                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/35374" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/125971.png" alt="Victorious Boxers: Revolution"> <span>Victorious Boxers: Revolution</span></a> | Wii     | [Sports - Boxing](https://retroachievements.org/hub/9123) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic NeonPug %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Whynot15 %}

Are you a boxing fan looking to spar a few rounds on the Wii but [Punch-Out](https://retroachievements.org/game/171) just isn't cutting it? Give Victorious Boxers: Revolution a shot! Victorious Boxers is based on the wildly successful and long-running [Hajime no Ippo](https://retroachievements.org/hub/23030) manga/anime series. The series primarily follows Ippo, a young man who goes from a nobody to a rising prodigy in the heavyweight boxing world, along with a fairly large cast of friends, rivals, and other boxers looking to stake their claim in boxing.

Unlike Punch-Out, this game is a true boxing sim, albeit a fairly arcadey one. You need to work on your spacing and use bobs, weaves, and ducks to avoid and throw in your own mixup of punching combos when an opening presents itself. There's a decent roster of characters with fairly distinct stats, styles, and special punches that can turn the tide of a fight in an instant.

The story mode is fairly lengthy, covering the major fights within the first 300-ish chapters of the manga. While the focus is on our main guy Ippo, various supporting characters get quite a share of the fights here as well. The fights also include various cutscenes during certain instances replicating iconic scenes from the source materials. The fights typically range from extremely easy to a fair challenge, although be prepared for a few very sudden difficulty spikes (be prepared to get to know Mashiba very, very well).

Unfortunately, there's not much to the game beyond the story mode, although the set tries to remedy this with a few challenge fight setups in the sparring mode. And the motion controls the game heavily touts are a very challenging way to go through the game (I highly recommend the GC controller option for better precision). Still, for fans of Ippo or boxing in general, this is probably one of the best "true" boxing titles offered on the Wii.
