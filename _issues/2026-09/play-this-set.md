## Medal of Honor: Underground (Game Boy Advance)

* Set by: {% rauserpic JosephTheCat %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Ezezin %}

This is a port of the [original PS1 game](https://retroachievements.org/game/11294) for a much, much weaker console in terms of hardware. Instead of making an adaptation, the studio decided it was a good idea to port the original FPS 3D experience to the Game Boy Advance. And yes, the result was as bad as it sounds.

We all know this game is trash, one of the worst experiences for the Game Boy Advance. It may hurt your eyes, enemies are mere huge pixels, sometimes walls may glitch, it runs on 10 FPS or even less, autoaim is mandatory but it also does not work correctly, enemies sometimes won't shoot or may kill you in seconds, the shotgun is useless... It's THE game for any masochist gamer who wants a real challenge or to try something new.

I really don't know why I like this game, so much in fact that I played it two times now! Maybe because of [Rebellion](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7401)'s attempt at porting a FPS on a handheld? Or probably because it’s so bad it’s good? Whatever the reason, you should totally try this out, if only just for 5 minutes to see for yourself this piece of "history" that shouldn't have been released in the first place, but they did it anyway.

And on top of that, the set made by {% rauserpic JosephTheCat %} is just perfect, adding more challenges on top of the already challenging experience and new interesting ways of finishing the stages, improving quite a lot on what the game has to offer that (almost) makes you forget how bad looking this is.

## Cartoon Network Racing (Nintendo DS)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Console     | Genre                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/9734" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/110039.png" alt="Cartoon Network Racing"> <span>Cartoon Network Racing</span></a> | Nintendo DS | [Kart Racing](https://retroachievements.org/hub/928) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Excessiveiser %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Laoth %}

Cartoon Network Racing is a crossover of the most famous cartoons from CN (Missing Ed, Edd n Eddy) for the Nintendo DS. While it's pretty much a pocket edition of the PS2 version, that doesn't mean this game is something to ignore. Most players took their time to beat it during the [75 Years of F1 RAcing](https://retroachievements.org/event/220-75-years-of-f1-racing-evergreen) event. I found it pretty fun to play, and I think more players should take a look at this hidden wacky kart racing gem.

While the game might look easy, it is actually a challenge to master it. The touch screen minigames can be very hard with a mouse, but playing on a touch screen device makes those achievements a walk in the park (or should I say race in the park?). The time trials are the most difficult and time consuming part of the game, but hey, that cool Johnny Bravo badge is worth it. The other challenging part is beating the cups as specific characters that might not suit your playstyle. No, not all racers are the same; some are heavy, others have better turning, etc. By the time you master this set, you can claim that you are a master racer for all playstyles.

With DSi emulation having become easier to set up recently, I think it's a good time to look at what the console has to offer. Such as Rabi Laby, a 2D puzzle platformer with built-in speedrun goals!

At its core, Rabi Laby is a very simple game. Control two characters with their own abilities, and get through various puzzle and timing-based levels. A simplistic system like this forms a great basis for some casual speedrunning, with each level having medals to earn for speed.

As far as the set itself goes, it mostly focuses on the Platinum times, and has a few extra achievements for some levels. Again, simple but very effective.

With that in mind, maybe one day we can see a set for the sequel, Rabi Laby 2 as well. Maybe a Wish This Set is in order? Is the order a rabbit?

## Togepi no Daibouken (Pokemon Mini)

I played this game some time ago, and I was surprised by how much they added in for a console that mostly has games with simple
mechanics. In this, you guide the Togepi by rolling like in [Marble Madness](https://retroachievements.org/hub/20294) or [Super Monkey Ball](https://retroachievements.org/hub/1044), so if you know those games, then you know what to expect. It's very lenient in terms of difficulty; basically, there are 3 areas with levels that are cleared in less than 3 minutes each. You roll the Togepi to reach the stairs to take you down while dealing the usual obstacles you’d expect: ice floors, breaking tiles, bumpers, etc.

Since the levels are so short compared to bigger games, you can improve your times very quickly as you replay the same level multiple times to get better. Also, the set added specific challenges into particular levels for those who look for added challenge. For such a small console, it's one of the most polished games I’ve found in it.

## Pokémon Trozei! (Nintendo DS)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Console     | Genre                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/16294" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/148041.png" alt="Pokémon Trozei!"> <span>Pokémon Trozei!</span></a> | Nintendo DS | [Tile Matching](https://retroachievements.org/hub/9602) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic blendedsea %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Frenchy70 %}

When looking for DS touch screen only games to play on melonDS on my Android phone, I was surprised to see Pokémon Trozei! in the hub. I remember hearing about it from a [DYKG](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCyS4xQE6DK4_p3qXQwJQAyA) video covering it as the lowest selling Pokemon series game, and remembering it just having bizarre bosses and a comic book pop art style not seen in any other game in the franchise. Low and behold when I played it for myself, I found a pretty solid puzzle game that integrates the Pokemon catching mechanic in a shockingly fun way, even more so than in the [Pokemon Pinball](https://retroachievements.org/game/789) titles in my opinion.

The setup is that you play as a female spy, Lucy Fleetfoot, who is infiltrating storage facilities and taking down super villains through hacking, to recover the stolen Pokemon that their evil organization is capturing to harness their collective energy. You need to match 4 of the same Pokemon together to start a chain, where every subsequent connection must have 3 in a row, and then 2 in a row. You need to keep doing so to stay afloat from the rapidly rising Pokemon that will end the stage if left unmanaged. Boss fights throw in gimmicks that will mess up your setup for chains if you don’t keep them stunned for long enough. They can range from something as simple as shuffling your board to more annoying ones like taking a Pokemon from a chain that’s about to go off or making 90% of the board go dark.

The Pokedex is where this game gets more interesting, as each stage, mode, and even save file has their own rare/secret pokemon to acquire by making a connection with them while clearing boards. The set does a good job with separating the save file dependent ones as individual achievements, with the dev providing the save files that the specific legendary is guaranteed to spawn on. Because of that and the Pokedex including most of everything up to gen 4, this makes it one of the hardest Pokedexes to complete in the series.

## Madou Monogatari I (Genesis/Mega Drive)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Console            | Genre                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/2392" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/068517.png" alt="Madou Monogatari I"> <span>Madou Monogatari I</span></a> | Genesis/Mega Drive | [Dungeon Crawl](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5757) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Quenthel %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Tanukitsune %}

Dungeon crawling can be daunting, even intimidating, but what if there was a perfect "My First Dungeon Crawler"? Madou Monogatari I could be that game!

You may have heard of [Puyo Puyo](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5667), one of the most popular puzzle games next to [Tetris](https://retroachievements.org/hub/835), but its origins are in the [Madou Monogatari](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8366) series. The Puyos are basically the slime of the game, and you'll recognize many of the enemies as Puyo Puyo opponents. Just take a minute to think about it. Imagine if [Dragon Quest](https://retroachievements.org/hub/6436) made a puzzle game with their slimes and that became more popular than the original series.

You play as Kindergarten Arle, who must pass a test by conquering a dungeon. This is a special test dungeon, with not only monsters and treasures, but questions you must answer by putting the right item in the allotted slot. Some are clear, "Put a diamond", others.... not so much. They will ask you for items that come from merging, so you must take notes not only of the questions, but of the fusions they ask for. You're likely to already own the item they ask for, but since you haven't done the merging, you don't know yet.

Combat is input based. Imagine [Street Fighter](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8335) moves, but you have to learn them first. The first spell they teach is Diacute, which boosts the next spell cast, and you can cast Diacute a few times for a devastating spell.

The game does not have many traps, and the hidden areas are not that hard to find either, which is why I recommend this game as your first dungeon crawler.

Another reason is the presentation. Not only is this one of the cutest dungeon crawlers, it's also one of the funniest. Some of the secrets, spells, and monsters are the most hilarious things I've ever seen. Arle's airheadedness makes her adorable and funny and makes the game a joy to play.

As for the test itself? There are 40 chests, 40 questions, and 20 hidden points you need to earn. You need NINETY points to get a passing grade. The hidden points are mostly from helping people, usually by casting the right spell.

The game is very beginner friendly and a joy to play, so if you want a simple and funny dungeon crawler, you can't do better than Madou Monogatari.

## Silent Debuggers (PC Engine/TurboGrafx-16)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Console                 | Genre                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/2324" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/119459.png" alt="Silent Debuggers"> <span>Silent Debuggers</span></a> | PC Engine/TurboGrafx-16 | [First-Person Shooter](https://retroachievements.org/hub/3942), [Survival Horror](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8328) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic DionOf93 %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Dyhalto %}

Silent Debuggers is a peculiar name. It sounds more like it'd be a cyberpunk mystery than a survival horror set on a space station.

You play as yourself in the first-person perspective. You and your partner Leon are Debuggers, a cross between bounty hunters and scavengers, and the setting is a derelict space station full of abandoned cargo, up for the taking. It's also full of monsters, and every previous Debugger who went in with the same intentions was never heard from again.

Leon takes a seat in the control room and provides logistical support while you handle the dirty work, cleaning out the infestation using a myriad of impressive weapons ranging from laser cannon to homing missile launcher. Naturally, as you progress deeper into the bowels of the station, you learn the origin of the monsters and the truth of what happened to the original crew.

What makes Silent Debuggers so engrossing is the atmosphere of dread it cultivates using both environment and gameplay. There's no BGM except during cutscenes. All you hear are your echoing footsteps, the slow, raspy breathing of the monsters, and the rapid drone of your sensor as something closes in. Gameplay-wise, the Outer blocks are where the action happens, with the Inner block being a mostly-safe place to retreat, but if it gets invaded, then you have to haul ass to where the monster is and kill it, or else you risk losing one of eight Inner blocks as the station auto-ejects it to prevent further "contamination". Those blocks don't come back on the next floor; they're gone forever. If the station ejects the Sensor Control block, your sensor will become erratic for the rest of the game. Heaven forbid you lose the Battery Recharging block (although a workaround does exist).

This is the survival horror genre in its infancy, five years before [Resident Evil](https://retroachievements.org/game/29328) coined the phrase. Don't go thinking this is some historic novelty, though. This is the type of game you wish you could forget so you could re-experience the panic and desperation of your first playthroughs.

## Germs: Nerawareta Machi (PlayStation)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Console     | Genre                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/18261" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/152421.png" alt="Germs: Nerawareta Machi"> <span>Germs: Nerawareta Machi</span></a> | PlayStation | [Adventure](https://retroachievements.org/hub/2650), [Survival Horror](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8328) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Layton %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpicalt Femcel, Grungust %}

Germs is one of the most obscure, fascinating, and unhinged experimental titles in the entire PlayStation 1 library. Released in 1999 exclusively in Japan as an indie title, it’s a surreal first-person open world mystery game that was doing weird sandbox gameplay long before the gaming world really had a name for it.

You play as an investigative reporter returning to your hometown of Kousaka, only to find the city slowly being overtaken by a bizarre biological threat: alien "Germs" that are mutating the locals and infecting the environment. What makes Germs so captivating isn't smooth action or polished mechanics, it's the sheer atmosphere. You navigate a fully rendered 3D city in real time, whether on foot, by bus, or driving around in your own car. There are lots of NPCs you meet, and every character you kill has a little note about them, even the alive construction vehicles. 

Being killed by a mutant is also not the end. You wake up in their base and can talk with mutants because you are one of them now. The game locks you out of progressing until you reverse the infection, but it lets you find more lore until then.

The combat itself is not that great. You circle strafe around enemies while spamming your weapon. The map can be a bit confusing, and the town's eerie silence can feel downright oppressive, but once you lock into its strange, hypnotic rhythm, there is truly nothing else quite like it on the PS1.

As for the RetroAchievements set, it turns this title into an absolute blast to explore. The achievements do a fantastic job guiding you through the obscure side quests, unique NPC interactions, and hidden lore details you’d almost certainly miss on a blind playthrough. The set encourages you to fully immerse yourself in Kousaka City, trying to find everything the game has to offer. Some events are time-based, which can be a bit confusing at first, but it isn't that bad.

If you have an appetite for a game which takes lots of inspiration from random conspiracy theories like weather manipulation machines, ESPs, and alien invasions, and don't mind not so great gameplay, Germs is a fun little experience waiting to be uncovered.

## Doraemon: Nobita to 3 Tsu no Seireiseki (Nintendo 64)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Console     | Genre                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/10593" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/076341.png" alt="Doraemon: Nobita to 3 Tsu no Seireiseki"> <span>Doraemon: Nobita to 3 Tsu no Seireiseki</span></a> | Nintendo 64 | [3D Platforming](https://retroachievements.org/hub/2952), [Action-Adventure](https://retroachievements.org/hub/2652), [Collect-a-thon](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7185) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic SlashTangent %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpicalt ChoccyMilk, ChocoMilk %}

Doraemon: Nobita to 3 Tsu no Seireiseki (or "Doraemon: Nobita and the Three Spirit Stones") is a simple 3D platformer for the N64 based on a long-running manga and anime series from Japan. Naturally, given the obscurity of the game there's no English translation patch currently, but there are some excellent guides to walk players through how to play the game. For the most part, it's a basic 3D platformer with some light run & gun shooting of enemies, with the ability to switch characters and use vehicles from time to time.

Most of the experience consists of wandering around large, somewhat empty early 3D environments looking for where to go, potentially stumbling upon chests containing mostly useless items to collect. The game has an odd feeling to it that reminds me of liminal spaces, where the player has a vague overall objective of helping fairies and unfreezing a king or something, but very little direction about how to do so. Unless they're strictly following a guide or even if they are, a significant portion of playtime is spent simply wandering around the huge levels, finding the next thing to interact with. With overly bright, cheery music in most areas, it's an odd contrast, one that I personally found comforting. I would hesitate to call this a fun game, but it's a uniquely compelling experience.

The 10-12 levels in the game are accessed through touching various random sections in a small beachside hub, which aren't really labeled, so it's up to player discovery to figure out where to go. Items that are found in chests or from bosses are sometimes required to progress, usually with context clues given about where they're needed. Some of the items are confusingly only received if the player plays through certain levels again. The bosses are quite easy with the exception of the final boss, who rightfully packs a modest punch. Overall though, the game is super easy, fitting for something young kids would probably be playing.

Thankfully, the achievements are simply for collecting 100% of the items in the game, which is trivial with a guide. For fans of 3D platformers and especially those that are into early 3D with its vast, empty sort of liminal spaces, I think this game will be an interesting treat. Hopefully the two N64 sequels also receive achievements at some point. It looks like they're more sophisticated 3D action-adventure games with extra puzzle solving added in the mix.

## Illbleed (Dreamcast)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Console   | Genre                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/7118" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/049903.png" alt="Illbleed"> <span>Illbleed</span></a> | Dreamcast | [Action](https://retroachievements.org/hub/836), [Horror](https://retroachievements.org/hub/6658) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Soulsderfuchs %}, {% rauserpicalt Darky, DarkyAndreas %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic LeeD %}

I never owned a Dreamcast growing up, and by the time I even played one, I just assumed that thing had no real games outside of [Sonic](https://retroachievements.org/game/3416) and [Crazy Taxi](https://retroachievements.org/game/3399), and brushed it off. As I've dug deeper into its limited library, though, I've found that some of my all time favorite games were released on that thing, and I had no Idea. Illbleed is one of those gems.

Illbleed is a game whose mechanics are hard to describe, but also at the end of the day almost don't even matter for the purposes of enjoying it. It is every campy horror movie you've ever watched put into one, complete with bad "acting", cheesy monsters, gruesome deaths, and eccentric characters. The premise is that you and your friends are awarded free tickets to the brilliantly named "Illbleed" haunted amusement park, and are invited to take part in a competition. If you can survive, you'll be awarded large sums of cash! Talk about an easy setup for a horror movie! However, Illbleed takes it a step further. Not only is it one horror movie idea, it's many. The amusement park is actually a varied selection of themed horror movie trope attractions: a killer department store, a kid turned abomination/killer, puppets made of wood out to kill you, and more! Conquer all of them, solve the overarching mystery of the park itself, save your friends, and escape ILLBLEED!

As a game itself, Illbleed has some very hit or miss mechanics, and is likely the type of game where you'll need to read the manual or look up some info on what to actually do. The general idea is that you have to navigate each horror themed level, solve whatever mystery there is, and often fight a final boss related to the story. As part of that you are given a scanner that lets you scan anomalies at the cost of your sanity, lest that anomaly become a jump scare (yes jump scares are actual mechanics within the park itself) and drain much more of your sanity. That, along with monster battles that lock you into a small arena to either fight or flee (think [Resident Evil](https://retroachievements.org/hub/6889) combat only stuck within a small space), cause Illbleed to become something of a resource management game, making sure you have enough recovery items to top off your health and sanity as they drain. It is definitely a game you just have to play to really understand, but once you get the gist of what the game wants you to do, you can just bask in the campiness it presents! The Toy Story ripoff level had myself and some friends absolutely hysterical with how ridiculous it was, I will never forget that.

The set itself is great! {% rauserpic Soulsderfuchs %} and {% rauserpicalt Darky, DarkyAndreas %} did a great job fleshing out the set with just enough challenge to keep you on your toes, but nothing that feels impossible. It also serves as a great reminder of content that you would not expect if you just played through it blind. I'm happy it was chosen for the [Halloween event](https://retroachievements.org/event/194-halloween-2025-forest-of-demotion-evergreen), as I would not have played it otherwise, but I still think it’s a critically slept on gem of a game!

I personally think I will be playing this game every Halloween, in the same way some folks watch their favorite campy horror movies. I highly recommend this game to anyone who enjoys horror even a little bit!
