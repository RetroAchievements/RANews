* Write-up by: {% rauserpic CheeseKnight %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/116/1168075470/e299375d4c66438c6c7d67205e9806fb5f73ccf0/01KR1M6067WGTVY2VYJQ3EBY90-lg-webp.webp">

There is a distinct lack of sports game sets on RetroAchievements, despite being one of the most popular genres in gaming. This is hopefully changing, as there have been a few [NFL](https://retroachievements.org/hub/18878) sets released recently, and I hope Madden NFL 2004 joins them some day. I've poured hundreds of hours into various NFL games over the years, but Madden 04 stands out as the most memorable iteration of the franchise. It has many of the fun customization options and franchise mode features that the N64-era games lacked, while still maintaining a faster-paced arcade style of play compared to the more realistic modern-day releases. The cover athlete, Falcons quarterback Michael Vick, is probably the most broken sports game character since [Tecmo Bowl](https://retroachievements.org/game/21446) Bo Jackson. A 95 speed rating meant defenses were toast if he escaped the pocket. The owner mode is my favorite addition to the game. Being able to tinker with off the field options like ticket and concession prices, coaching staff management, and even relocating the franchise adds another level of immersion to the game. It's cool to see how your on the field success (or lack thereof) affects attendance and fan support.

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Riley101 %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/149/149383886/288a3ef5cb179e57006e08331f84b1c805b05cd0/288a3ef5cb179e57006e08331f84b1c805b05cd0.png">

Being in middle school and having gotten my first DSi points card for my birthday, I was elated to find a game that spoke to my desire for fame and stardom. This game is an enhanced port of a Java phone game, and its inclusion on the system really helped to flesh it out into more of a fun-filled game. There are so many intricacies you're able to do in this game as you attempt to work your way through a Fame Academy-esque competition show - making friends (or enemies!) to further your career, performing on guitar, vocals, or drums to wow the crowds (including a cover of Avril Lavigne's "Girlfriend"), and engaging with a rival who's hell-bent on making sure you don't make it to the top! The game's filled with several pop-culture references, and even if they're extremely dated, I still think they add a nice, humorous touch to the game. The game also has one of my favorite features - character customization! You can pick out some cute outfits with several different color palettes to choose from, and even have the opportunity to purchase additional outfits in the mall! What's more, the game also has a trophy system, allowing you to keep track of your merits and high points throughout the journey to the top. I know that this game is a very deep cut, but trust me when I say it's a fun, campy trip filled with reality TV drama, catfights, and just a little bit of supernatural influence... But I don't want to give too much away! Play the game yourself, and see why it's such an iconic hidden gem on the DSi!

## Killzone: Liberation (PlayStation Portable)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Console              | Genre                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/3553" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/118377.png" alt="Killzone: Liberation"> <span>Killzone: Liberation</span></a> | PlayStation Portable | [Third-Person Shooter](https://retroachievements.org/hub/3876) |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Beliskner999 %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/136/1363307214/14f817fcafdb06db3b861e983137d6a5b04226ac/backfill-b7t0g8801cjh8Fymfip.png">

This game truly is one of the greats from the PSP. Unlike its counterparts on the home consoles, this is not an FPS, but rather a top-down shooter with a heavy emphasis on tactics. A wise choice considering how poorly so many other FPS games fared on Sony's new handheld. It was one of my most played games in the mid 00s, and what I'll never forget is magazines at the time teasing me for the better part of the year claiming a sequel was coming. Despite its immense success, it never materialized.

It functions extremely well as a portable experience, with tight levels that give you quick, but challenging fun on the go. While you won't have to contest with nearly as many enemies as you do in the PS2 game, you better bring your A game, because the Helghast have not come to play and will give you a tough time even on the lowest difficulty. Effective use of cover and the arsenal you are provided are key to progressing through the campaign, amazing boss fights included. Every once in a while you are thrown in a vehicle, which gives you the chance for some unkind revenge against now mostly defenseless enemies. You can also buy weapon upgrades or have a friend join in the fun in co-op to make the higher difficulties more manageable. Trust me, you'll need all the help you can get.

Aside from the campaign, you can also prove your skills in a variety of different challenges that you unlock by playing through the main story. From target practice over base defending to timed fetch quests, you are presented with several hours of spectacular additional gameplay through which you can collect 24 super shiny gold medals. With all this content, this game is a prime candidate for an achievement set, and over 100 people who also already requested a set agree. It simply has everything. It looks great, it sounds great, it plays great. What more could you ever want?

## Bubble and Squeak (Genesis/Mega Drive)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Console            | Genre              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/4177" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/071460.png" alt="Bubble and Squeak"> <span>Bubble and Squeak</span></a> | Genesis/Mega Drive | Puzzle Platforming |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Ezezin %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/401/401500878/18546ae75e9f355b84fdb2377d89298cf0c50758/18546ae75e9f355b84fdb2377d89298cf0c50758.png">
<figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

Bubble and Squeak is a hidden and underrated gem. Don't get fooled by its cute appearance, however. The difficulty spikes a lot after the first 4 levels.

This game has one objective: find Squeak (the bipedal blue monster), and take him with you to the exit (a red and white pole) in a way that both characters shake hands. Yeah, that is how you end a level, don't ask.

Sounds pretty simple, right? Well, besides the monsters and other hazards, you have to be careful with your partner, because it will follow you everywhere unless ordered not to, and can't jump on its own except when near a ledge. While this may sound like yet another game with an escorting mission, this is one of the few cases where it works perfectly, since all levels and game mechanics were made with these limitations in mind. You can tell Squeak to follow you or to stay in place, both required to progress through the levels, and sometimes you might find a bubblegum machine which, after putting 3 coins (obtained from enemies), will allow you to mount the blue monster and gain more abilities, like wings!

Also, the bonus game, which is a horizontal shoot 'em up, is also hard and pretty unique in the sense that it has its own progression separate from the main game. In this bonus game, you control Bubble and Squeak in a submarine that must reach to the end of the different stages and defeat a cat that is also in a submarine. Of course, just like the main game, it is not easy, since the camera moves from left to right on its own and never stops, so you can't stay at the left side of the screen all the time or you might get crushed by a wall, but you also must watch out for the enemies and other hazards that comes from the right.

Just don't get caught exploring for too long, because after a while the water will rise constantly until you lose or reach the exit, and yes, you and the blue monster can drown just like Sonic. The duration greatly depends on difficulty, and there is no way to recover oxygen except reaching the surface.

I would love to see this game get a set someday. It is cute, hard, and unique for its era, and has so many possibilities for creating very interesting challenges and secrets in almost every level, rewarding those who take the time (but not too much) to explore every nook and cranny.

## Summoner 2 (PlayStation 2)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Console       | Genre      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ---------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/19072" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/075189.png" alt="Summoner 2"> <span>Summoner 2</span></a> | PlayStation 2 | Action RPG |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic TiOPA %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://cdn.mobygames.com/screenshots/15641795-summoner-2-gamecube-performing-a-combo-attack-on-a-creature.png">

Like many others, I grew up playing video games. To me, they were never just a source of entertainment, they were a way to connect with family and friends, creating memories that have stayed with me long after the games themselves were finished. Over the years, there have been countless games that hold a special place in my heart, but one in particular stands out above the rest.

At the time, my oldest brother and I were constantly at odds. We fought and bickered over the smallest, most ridiculous things, and eventually things became so bad that we stopped speaking to each other for several months. Then, on my birthday in October 2002, my brother woke me up that morning with a present in his hand. He gave me a hug and simply said, "I'm sorry."

The gift was Summoner 2 for the PlayStation 2.

We had always shared a love for RPGs, although by that point in his life, he had started to move away from gaming...That being said, we stayed up together that night and played Summoner 2 from start to finish.

What makes the game so special to me isn't necessarily the game itself; there are countless games out there with better stories, gameplay, etc. It is what it represented. It was an apology, a reconciliation, and a reminder of the bond my brother and I had always shared.

Even now, with me in my late 30s and my brother in his early 50s, Summoner 2 is something we still reminisce about. Whenever we talk about it, we're not just remembering a game, we're remembering that night, and the moment that helped bring two brothers back together.

For me, Summoner 2 will always be more than a video game, it is a memory of my brother, a turning point in our relationship, and a reminder that sometimes the simplest gestures can mean the most.

## Dai-4-Ji Super Robot Taisen (SNES/Super Famicom)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Console            | Genre                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/2756" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/028047.png" alt="Dai-4-Ji Super Robot Taisen"> <span>Dai-4-Ji Super Robot Taisen</span></a> | SNES/Super Famicom | [Tactical RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/3008) |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic gaaki %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/923/923888334/9af03dffcacc7e24dda9540c9625df1db183da0e/backfill-oa1jm36kh7bm6ifu6XL.png">
<figcaption></figcaption>

Crossovers are always beloved when multiple creators combine their characters into one scenario. They all celebrate each other by giving credit for inventions in a genre no one could've imagined before. Say, no one expected in the 90s that Nintendo would combine their characters into one [platform fighter](https://retroachievements.org/game/10082), or that [Cyclops from X-Men would shake hands with Ryu from Street Fighter](https://retroachievements.org/game/12266). It's not only notable for creators that such an idea lets them see a different approach, but for the fans themselves that they have a larger space to share with.

The [Super Robot Wars/Taisen](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7217) series is very notable when it comes to crossovers, by combining every popular Japanese mecha series into one game with multiple scenarios to play with in a classic tactical RPG format. No matter what their themes are, it says something when absurd and fantasy-leaning series share a room with a realistic, apocalyptic one that shows the horror of human beings. Starting in April 1991, the series now includes over 50 games, with 100+ mecha series shared in-between, and today many new games have an official localization in English. Which is, without a doubt, a great thing to see, but there are still so many to translate from the old days: Alpha, Z, and Compact series, F & F Final, so many standalone games like K, L, D, MX, Neo, and many more. And even more games to claim, to try to make achievements for.

That's why I'd like to recommend Dai-4-Ji Super Robot Taisen that was recently translated by none other than the [Aeon Genesis](https://aeongenesis.net/) team themselves. Published by [Banpresto](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5440) in 1995, the game features over 44 scenarios and over 20 series (counting Yoshiuki Tomino's, Go Nagai's, Ken Ishikawa's and Tadao Nagahama's works), with original characters like Masaki Ando, Ryune Zoldark, Mio Sasuga, and more that you might know from [Super Robot Taisen: Original Generation](https://retroachievements.org/game/5176). There is also a customizable main character with his customizable mecha, and you can color your own Gundam, for god's sake!

In the series' history, this game holds a special place, because without it we might have lost it (true story, [check it out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A51eY_p5w6c)). The game was my first serious approach with the mecha genre back in 2020, so I do have a warm space in my heart for it. So, I hope, you will too after playing this!
