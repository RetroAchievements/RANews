## <span class="tag"><span>Hack</span></span> MegaMari (NES/Famicom)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Console     | Genre                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/18308" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/069799.png"> <span><span class="tag"><span>Hack</span></span> MegaMari</span></a> | NES/Famicom | [2D Platforming](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8837) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Raeyene %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic PsyXchO %}

I decided to go with this game because it is featured in [The Unwanted](https://retroachievements.org/forums/topic/14787), and I definitely wanted to write about this set. It is probably one of the best [Mega Man 1](https://retroachievements.org/game/1448) hacks out there. The concept is simple; it’s basically a full reskin of Mega Man 1 using the [Touhou](https://retroachievements.org/hub/9234) character universe. If you're familiar with the first game of the series, then you will feel welcomed with this set. I do feel the difficulty is roughly the same or just slightly harder than the base game, so don’t worry about the hack being super difficult.

The Robot Masters and all the Wily stage bosses behave in the same way. A cool new addition the hacker did was alter the enemies’ weaknesses, making it feel a bit fresh even if you're familiar with the base game. I was really surprised to see that. It was cool figuring out the bosses’ weaknesses again, after knowing the base game’s boss weaknesses. Maybe other people can have that same kind of feeling as I did going through this wonderful hack!

Another thing the hacker changed was making the levels completely different. I was drawn to the game more and appreciated every aspect this game had. And honestly now I think about it, there aren’t many bottomless pits or spikes to watch out for. I know there are some, but it’s not that bad. Some levels feel a bit shorter, while others feel a bit longer. There isn't a good consistency there, but that's okay. The levels and themes still feel good. :)

Thanks to The Unwanted, I was able to experience this hidden gem of a game. I recommend this for any Mega Man fan, or even if you just have knowledge of the first game. It’s one of the best hacks I've played in a long time, and I feel that quite a few people will enjoy this game. We need more people playing this set. The set that the dev made is quite good. It has a lot of damageless achievements, but Mega Man doesn't have a lot to offer for any kind of better challenge, besides maybe speedrunning levels, but that feels off. So the dev made the best challenges they can offer!

## SD Gundam: Dimension War (Virtual Boy)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Console     | Genre                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/12720" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/059612.png" alt="SD Gundam: Dimension War"> <span>SD Gundam: Dimension War</span></a> | Virtual Boy | [Tactical RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/3008) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Maximdraco %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Whynot15 %}

SD Gundam: Dimension War is a crossover [Gundam](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7529) game featuring the teeny-tiny super deformed (SD) versions of all sorts of mobile suits you know and love. The story is…practically nonexistent. Your little fleet of smushed mecha and battleships fight against other groups, with the goal of taking out a specific unit or units to claim victory. Repeat 8 times and that’s the game.

While billing itself as a TRPG, the “tactics” elements are pretty light, and the “RPG” close to nonexistent. While some units have the option to attack indirectly or set up ambushes, most of the time you’ll just be advancing your units to get to the opposing mobile suits to start the “action” portion of combat. Here, your suit and the enemy suit take each other on one-on-one for about 1 minute. Each suit has a custom SD-ified sprite and weapon loadout, and weapons do indeed behave fairly differently from one another and showcase some of the more advanced mobile suit’s custom weapons, like Epyon’s whip and Z Gundam’s beam rifle. Despite the differences in weapons, the battles all follow pretty predictable, button-mashing patterns where the best tactic is getting your opponent stuck and semi-stun locked into a corner while you blast away.

This isn’t a crown jewel of Gundam games or the SD series, but there is fun to be had here. The game is pretty short - 8 levels, although the later stages take significantly longer thanks to larger teams of more advanced mobile suits. But if you’re a fan of the older Gundam series (I believe Gundam Wing suits are the “newest” series to make an appearance), you’ll enjoy smashing your suits against the opponent as if they were action figures.

## Toy Story 2: Buzz Lightyear to the Rescue! (PlayStation)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Console     | Genre                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/11357" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/060240.png" alt="Toy Story 2: Buzz Lightyear to the Rescue!"> <span>Toy Story 2: Buzz Lightyear to the Rescue!</span></a> | PlayStation | [3D Platforming](https://retroachievements.org/hub/2952), [Collect-a-thon](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7185) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic mario2188 %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Ezezin %}

This was the second game I played for [Collect-a-thon MaRAthon](https://retroachievements.org/forums/topic/35944), with recommendations on Discord that this was one of the quickest of the bunch, which is true if you’re are not aiming for mastery like I did.

The game was fine. It was fun and not that hard (you can farm lives very easily by just exiting the stage and re-entering). The rapid fire laser is way too overpowered, and it has so many charges that it would be rare for most players to run out of it even if shooting wildly at enemies, and besides 2 stages, that power-up is often in the same room as where you start or in a very close room.

Bosses aren't that hard either, except maybe the last 3 where you may need to learn their attack patterns and are immune to lasers, but they can always be beaten quite easily if you have full life. This is where the achievements shine the most, as the set does a great job at making most bosses an actual challenge, asking players to beat them in unintended ways instead of spin + tanking damage.

The story is the same as the movie: rescue Woody from Al. However, I feel the game would have been much better if it were its own thing, since the stages and even Buzz have barely any consistency with the movie, like Buzz trying to speak with command center. Why would you at this point?

Overall, for a licensed game, it is a good one, and I can see why it is so popular (at least in RA). However, it has not aged very well. The controls are a bit clunky, the camera almost never helps during bosses, and it is clear that it took the movie's story and scenes for easy marketing and recognition for children and parents during that time. Thankfully, {% rauserpic mario2188 %}’s set is pretty through, and while it is hard in some parts, I think it has that perfect balance of not being overly hard without being a cakewalk either, making the whole experience a lot better than just playing the game on its own.

## <span class="tag"><span>Hack</span></span> Antinomia: Final Fantasy VI (SNES/Super Famicom)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Console            | Genre                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/30917" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/102566.png"> <span><span class="tag"><span>Hack</span></span> Antinomia: Final Fantasy VI</span></a> | SNES/Super Famicom | [Turn-Based RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5468) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic solidification %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Jurbadis %}

Antinomia is a hack of [Final Fantasy VI](https://retroachievements.org/game/341) that I can easily recommend to any fans of the original game. It does not change any of the underlying mechanics, rather expanding the narrative by providing alternative viewpoints to the first part of the game, before going onto a hypothetical scenario in the second part. The new scenes are very well written, and you can see that a lot of care was put into every detail. There's even a playthrough on YouTube where the dev of the hack offers their commentary and explains their thought process for every change. I also highly recommend checking it out!

As for the main gameplay change, General Leo is now a playable character, and he is loads of fun to use. You'll get various glimpses of his power whenever the story focuses on him.

The set covers all the new scenes and offers some fun challenges, particularly in regards to defeating the various dragons. And you will be happy to hear that there is absolutely no rage farming in the achievement set, so you can enjoy the story without worrying about needing to have a spreadsheet open at all times. Mastering the set will require mostly one playthrough. Though, as is usual with FFVI, you'll need to make a backup save before the second part of the game.

Overall, I really liked this hack. It gives a fresh experience to the FFVI world, and I absolutely recommend any fans of the game to try it out, even if you are not normally into hacks.

## The Legend of Heroes: Trails in the Sky FC (PlayStation Portable)

| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/10479" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/038832.png" alt="The Legend of Heroes: Trails in the Sky FC"> <span>The Legend of Heroes: Trails in the Sky FC</span></a> | PlayStation Portable | [Turn-Based RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5468) |

* Set by: {% rauserpicalt tele, televandalist %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic StingX2 %}

I really had my doubts that I would buy so heavily into this series. Even with the sales pitch of it having the MCU setup of RPGs building up to its own endgame, I just had doubts. I was asked to review this back in its original launch, and having just got off another 100 hour RPG, passed pretty easily on this. I didn't think about it again until the RA set, and I still put it off. Then, I finally dipped my toes, and I was treated to an on-foot pilgrimage around this world, helping each region as you go with your Bracer duties (think typical helper guild stuff in fiction).

What I wasn't expecting was how quickly I'd fall in love with this world and its NPCs that just feel alive. Everything feels like it has purpose, and that's something I can name very few series that have nailed correctly. Featuring a turn-based battle system and one of the most electric party casts this side of [Persona](https://retroachievements.org/hub/2763), it truly is a game in which every character you meet becomes a beloved or hated character because they all are that memorable.

The RA set is a beautiful crafted gem of a set that challenges you to squeeze all the content out of it while also being a battle master by never falling in battle. Too many RPGs let you sleep through battle systems, but making that cost you achievements really makes you focus on the battles themselves. Trails is much like Mass Effect 1 to me, as this introductory chapter that introduces you to the setting, the lore, and provides you with a fantastic journey around the world.

## Ys: The Oath in Felghana (PlayStation Portable)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Console              | Genre                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/3132" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/124081.png" alt="Ys: The Oath in Felghana"> <span>Ys: The Oath in Felghana</span></a> | PlayStation Portable | [Action RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8625) |

* Set by: {% rauserpicalt tele, televandalist %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic cbrx %}

[Ys](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8265) is a long-running Action RPG series by [Nihon Falcom](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7991), featuring a fiery red-haired swordsman named Adol Christin. Our protagonist set out and recorded 100 different travelogues before he retired of old age, but so far only 10 of those have been playable in the mainline series, with the series being up to Ys X (Proud Nordics) at the time of writing.

While the series has experimented with various types of combat during its nearly 40-year run, some of which are the Bump Combat system, the Standard combat featured in this game, and team-based / RPS systems later, this engine and combat system is definitely my favorite. It is snappy, responsive, and just feels satisfying to play, but you also aren't as overpowered as in later entries, with the Flash Guard and Flash Move systems making you (near) invincible with good play.

The Oath in Felghana is a remake of [Wanderers from Ys](https://retroachievements.org/game/13982) (aka Ys III), and for me, the definitive version of the game. It features well-written characters and some fantastic character interactions, a bangin' OST as expected from Falcom Sound Team during this time period, and smooth combat with a fantastic difficulty selection from Very Easy to Inferno. You can expect Nightmare and Inferno to have some really difficult, but ultimately satisfying bosses throughout the game (definitely stick to something a bit lower on your first playthrough, though). If you want to get started with Ys, this is a perfect starting point, since the games are mostly self-contained and stories that are more heavy on the writing and worldbuilding appear later in the series.

I don't want to give away more specifics because I believe these games are best enjoyed fully blind, and they aren't too long either. I would expect a new player to take maybe 10 hours to beat the game, and then if they want to get into the nitty-gritty, they can choose to approach NG+ and the harder difficulties.

Who knows? Maybe you will also end up falling in love with these games and become the next member of Falcom Gang™.

## Power Soukoban (SNES/Super Famicom)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Console            | Genre                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/3389" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/150087.png" alt="Power Soukoban"> <span>Power Soukoban</span></a> | SNES/Super Famicom | [Sokoban](https://retroachievements.org/hub/20333) |

* Set by: {% rauserpic Kayesay %}
* Write-up by: {% rauserpic DantePlays %}

While I don’t think box-pushing [Sokoban](https://retroachievements.org/hub/20333) games are for everyone (they're not generally for me!), Power Soukoban has enough twists on the original concept that I enjoyed playing it all the way through and solving 100% of the puzzles, even the ones not on the critical path.

There are a few things that make Power Soukoban a different and more compelling take on the genre. Firstly, when you push a rock onto its mark, it goes away. The marks are holes in the ground that you’re filling up and closing, so it’s harder to block yourself into a dead end and need to restart. Secondly, your character has the ability to shoot out a guided fireball (sort of like the remote missile from [Metal Gear](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7530)) at any time after a brief charge-up, and this can be steered around to push blocks you can’t otherwise physically get behind, opening up the puzzle designs to be more varied and interesting. Thirdly, the holes you need to fill with rocks are constantly emitting monsters. They aren't really that dangerous, and mostly just get in your way, but their presence does add a sense of urgency and tension to what otherwise could be some very slow-paced puzzle rooms.

Each puzzle you solve gives you some rewards, which can either be XP toward getting more health, XP toward getting stronger fireballs, or ammo for a special weapon that the boss of that zone will be weak to. Yes, there are boss fights, which you aren’t really prepared for, as none of the other combat in the game is at all challenging, but the special weapon ammo shreds the bosses so quickly that they’re not a problem. If the bosses are too easy for you, the achievement set provides an incentive for beating them without the special weapons, which turns these simple encounters into major challenges.

Overall, Power Soukoban is a terrific game and I had a great time with it. Yes, whenever I entered a new room after a difficult puzzle I would sort of groan a little, but then I’d pause (thankfully they let you look at the puzzle while paused), look over the situation, make a plan, and get to work. If you at all enjoy puzzle games, I can easily recommend trying this one out.

This is some 90's slop right here...Which is the main reason I recommend playing this set.

The bonus laughs are thanks to the achievement names. Commandeer the backhoe and bust out your jackhammer for this peak material.
