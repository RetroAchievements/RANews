## All-Star Fighters (PlayStation 2)

<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/468/4680398/3b6611c5248879743866b1e2105402ed5fb65b9f/3b6611c5248879743866b1e2105402ed5fb65b9f-lg-webp.webp">

Fighting game crossovers are a beloved tradition among fans, with many famed entries like the [Capcom vs.](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7838) series and the many iterations of [Smash Bros](https://retroachievements.org/hub/1386). They celebrate the vibrancy and history of fighting games and sometimes give us a different glimpse of fan favorites and less-known characters in a different setting. All-Star Fighters is a hidden gem in the crossover tradition.

Published by [D3](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5266) in Japan and by the aptly named Essential Games in Europe in 2007, All-Star Fighters brings characters from the [Simple-2000](https://retroachievements.org/hub/3109) budget game series to the arena and is a must-play for fans of the bizarre and the obscure. With characters from [Monster Attack](https://retroachievements.org/game/19095), the [OneChanbara](https://retroachievements.org/hub/30242) series, and the many bikini babes that populate the Simple-2000 games, the break-out character is certainly Namedethgar, the main character from The Tokusatsu Henshin Hero who looks like a giant yellow slug, has several "wiggle" attacks, and one of his moves is called the "Wetty Pheromone".

As a 3D fighter, and considering the series that it originates from, it meets its expectations: a bad, shallow, budget fighter filled with bugs and exploits, and it gives a strong "We've got [Tekken](https://retroachievements.org/hub/994) at home" vibe. The graphics are pretty good and colorful though, and it is a genuinely funny game, and a great oddity to bring out during a gaming session among friends. A guaranteed good time!

## The Lord of the Rings: The Third Age (Game Boy Advance)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Console          | Genre                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/7381" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/131069.png" alt="The Lord of the Rings: The Third Age"> <span>The Lord of the Rings: The Third Age</span></a> | Game Boy Advance | [Strategy](https://retroachievements.org/hub/3013), Tactical RPG |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic crrool %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/204/2045471438/0977e33622ce9763d675748269b09c478157537b/0977e33622ce9763d675748269b09c478157537b.png">
<figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

Lord of the Rings: The Third Age is a fantastic turn-based strategy game for the GBA. At the start of the game you're offered a selection of good or evil. Which you choose determines your objectives for each of the 32 levels available. Once selected, you'll have to decide on your main hero: Aragorn, Gandalf, or Elrond for good, or Saruman, the Witch King, or the Mouth of Sauron for evil. These heroes will join in every level from that moment onward.

Every level is set up in much the same way. There are 3 flanks. Each flank is given a number of turns based on which heroes are present on each flank. This means rather than moving every character, you'll have to pick which ones to use that turn. This is also in large part what sets this turn-based strategy game apart from most. Your commanders matter. Every level has objectives and points tied to said objectives. Reach enough points and you win the level. Additionally, if you kill every opponent, you also win the level. This could make for interesting achievements as some levels have a "survive for x turns" objective, but it's entirely possible to beat every opponent before that turn. Levels can be replayed as often as you'd like, allowing for very few achievements to be missable.

In almost every level you select additional heroes. There are 8 different ones available for good and 8 for evil. If you play in Sauron mode these are extra valuable, as once they die, they die forever. If they all die you get a commander, which is much weaker and gives fewer moves per turn. One challenge achievement could require killing off every hero in the first level and then doing the entire game with just commanders.

Lastly, every hero, both main and extras, have items. You buy these with XP, gained by killing units on the flank of the hero (doubled if it's the hero himself doing the kill). Some items are consumables, and some are permanent buffs.

For any dev interested in picking up this game, here's a way the set could be structured:
- Achievement for each level (both good and evil)
- Achievement for certain levels beaten using a different requirement
- Beat the game on Sauron mode
- Beat the game on Grueling difficulty
- Beat the game using no consumables
- Beat the game without leaders alive
- Buy all upgrades on each hero

## Floigan Bros.: Episode 1 (Dreamcast)

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic RetroDronX %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/618/618621646/c5990e8568b94e36b8ef96d2a7e5190c34e73ef9/c5990e8568b94e36b8ef96d2a7e5190c34e73ef9-lg-webp.webp"><figcaption></figcaption>

Floigan Bros.: Episode 1 is a fun puzzle game made for the Sega Dreamcast in 2001. The story is about two brothers, Hoigle and Moigle, who want to save their junkyard from the evil Baron Malodorous. The player controls Hoigle. You must talk to your big brother Moigle and use his feelings to solve puzzles.

The game is very short and takes only one or two hours to finish. To win, you explore 13 areas, fight bad cats, and find seven hidden items. A good set for this game can include achievements for finishing the story, winning the "High Five" mini-game, and making Moigle happy, sad, angry, or scared. You can also add a challenge to beat the game quickly.

In short, Floigan Bros. is a forgotten but very cool Dreamcast game. New achievements will give retro players a great reason to play it again and enjoy its funny style.

## <span class="tag"><span>Hack</span></span> MOTHER: Rebound (SNES/Super Famicom)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Console            | Genre          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------ | -------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/37822" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/124056.png" alt=""> <span><span class="tag"><span>Hack</span></span> MOTHER: Rebound</span></a> | SNES/Super Famicom | Turn-Based RPG |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Jurbadis %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/140/1408199374/76adcb082a1f745556f4f5731f8ee20629502482/76adcb082a1f745556f4f5731f8ee20629502482.png">
<figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

There are a handful of [Earthbound hacks](https://retroachievements.org/hub/7884) out there, with varying degrees of quality. I am glad to report that this one stands out as one of the good ones! Put together by the not so small Team Psionics, you can see that a lot of love and hard work was put into the making of this hack. Set as a direct sequel to the base game, taking place 3 years in the future, MOTHER: Rebound follows the adventures of Nick, his robot Astro, and a handful of other friends on a search for Ness, who has mysteriously disappeared. While not the most imaginative of premises when it comes to romhack plots, it manages to tell a compelling story with emotional up and down beats, as you would expect from a [Mother](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8875) game.

It features a fantastic soundtrack, with both original songs and covers added into the mix. Without spoiling too much, I've got to say that I loved the rendition of Bohemian Rhapsody that serves as Onett's theme. It doesn't feel out of place, and it just fits very well. There are plenty of beautiful new sprites both for enemies and overworld characters as well. This hack also does something very creative with the party composition, giving you a party that is in practice larger than the usual 4, by making clever use of the guest party member slots.

As you would expect from a sequel romhack, it is interesting to see where various NPCs from the base game are placed and what their lives are like after the events of [Earthbound](https://retroachievements.org/game/264). It feels very rewarding to explore and find them throughout the world.

The hack is not that difficult and can be finished within a few hours. You level up really quickly due to an adjusted exp curve, but I believe that there is enough creative room for challenges during the boss fights. Overall, I had a great time in the 4 hours that I spent beating the hack, and I would love to see a set for it.

## One Piece: Become the Pirate King! (WonderSwan)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Console    | Genre                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/15444" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/094213.png" alt="One Piece: Become the Pirate King!"> <span>One Piece: Become the Pirate King!</span></a> | WonderSwan | [Turn-Based Strategy](https://retroachievements.org/hub/670) |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic RaphMec %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/118/1187736270/7d67ca245b6dfd46ba0786e5e1925667e861a95a/backfill-2npucda30q2a9YCd9ZN.png">
<figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

Being somewhat of a completionist (or perfectionist, in other words maybe somewhat crazy? - and given that a large number of people on RA chase video game achievements and masteries for fun, I am probably not alone in this), I often like not only completing everything a game has to offer, but also playing every game in a series in release order. Which brings us to the subject of this Wish This Set, the first [One Piece](https://retroachievements.org/hub/12246) video game to ever be released: One Piece: Become the Pirate King! That fact alone makes it very intriguing to many OP/Manga fans, but the game has several appealing elements aside from that.

The game was released in 2000 for the Japan-exclusive WonderSwan, which means that many people outside of Japan never got to experience it. The game is part board game, part turn-based strategy, part card game, part construction game. The gameplay advances in a system based on player turns (similar to board games). The player has to try to create a path for their ship using the tiles (or cards) that they pick. Combat in the game also uses these cards, and works as a 3v3 strategy fight with your favourite characters from the manga. Succeeding in a fight either advances the story, unlocks a new character, or unlocks some cool stuff for the glossary. The story follows Luffy from the start of the manga all the way up to the Loguetown arc (which is where the manga had reached at the time). Visually, the game looks beautiful and faithfully recreates the manga art.

I hope someone will consider making a set for this game one day. It truly merits a place on RA for any One Piece fan. Thank you for your time.

## Rise of the Guardians (Wii)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Console | Genre                                                                                                       |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic TheMicroMancer %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:right;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/748/748120782/e3698fbe9e425189ab8067f857f5222bee8a1b1c/01KNJJ5C46YWJ1TDCYZNG27WWX-lg-webp.webp">

Rise of the Guardians on the Wii is the video game based off the criminally underrated 2012 Dreamworks movie of the same name. It’s basically a hack & slash game where you explore all five Guardian realms to defeat Pitch's Nightmares from taking over, and in the process restore the Guardian's belief across the world.

In each of the Guardian's realms there are a bunch of objectives and missions for you to do, such as finding collectibles relating to each of the Guardians, freeing your friends from cages by defeating a wave of enemies, defeating as many enemies as you can on a time limit without your friend dying, and many other objectives. The game also has an in-game achievement list, filling a void for Wii players that did not have a Xbox 360 or PS3.

You can also make your characters stronger by purchasing gems that benefit them in various ways and use your skill points to increase Speed, Strength, Defense, etc. There is also a gallery where you can spend your spare gems on movie stills, concept art for the game, and art involving the movie itself.

You can beat the game in around 4 hours, and 100% in around 6 to 8 hours. It can feel somewhat repetitive and samey after playing it for a long while, but if you take your time with it, you could have a decent time with it. I say give this game a try if you adore the movie.

## Ryuukiheidan Danzarb \| Dragon Squadron: Danzarb (SNES/Super Famicom)

| Game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Console            | Genre                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| <a class="gameicon-link" href="https://retroachievements.org/game/23453" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <img class="gameicon" src="https://media.retroachievements.org/Images/083180.png" alt="Ryuukiheidan Danzarb \| Dragon Squadron: Danzarb"> <span>Ryuukiheidan Danzarb \| Dragon Squadron: Danzarb</span></a> | SNES/Super Famicom | [Turn-Based RPG](https://retroachievements.org/hub/5468) |

* Write-up by: {% rauserpic Prota %}

<figure style="text-align:center;float:left;width:50%;height:50%">
<img src="https://s3.retroachievements.org/game/screenshots/193/1938778830/ade2eea08aecaa9918ac96ac727665a22ae4a372/backfill-7rmgc2v75oqrd0eij2M.png">

Ryuukiheidan Danzarb is a game I didn't learn about until last year. One of my tasks in the [Rack It Up](https://retroachievements.org/forums/topic/24535) event was to search for an 8 or 16-bit era RPG to promote as a set I'd like to see get developed. At the time, I wanted to find something more niche and figured Sci-Fi JRPGs could use some love. That goes double for JRPGs that feature mechs, so that's pretty much where my search began. I ended up coming across Danzarb which was made by [Pandora Box](https://retroachievements.org/hub/8049), a lesser known developer, together with the highly acclaimed anime studio Gainax. From my research, I saw many people held the story in high regard. Going by the beginning prologue, I was already hooked. The only reason I stopped is because I wanted to wait for the game to hopefully get a set before I really sink my teeth into it. I've generally read that the narrative is full of twists and turns, and while there may be some cliches, it's a very Gainax story that hits some strong themes that don't pull any punches. Gameplay is more of a traditional turn-based RPG, and the game structure appears to be straightforward as far as I can tell. You get your story beats, proceed to mission, rinse and repeat. The missions have different objectives and feature puzzles as well. I believe, even with its more linear gameplay loop, that there's plenty of room for some fun and creative achievements. And did I mention you get to pilot robot dragons? I'd love for this game to get a set so that I can play beyond the first mission, as the prologue alone convinced me that I'd be in for a wild and emotional ride.
